The one-breath position

Category parity on AI-SPM, AI-DR and red teaming — plus the two things the US / EU / Israel field structurally cannot offer regulated APAC: sovereignty and validated evidence.

Never claim the attack graph is unique — several rivals ship one, and CISOs will check. We win on provable rigour (closed-loop red-team validation with evidence attached), the right to sell where others cannot (residency, on-prem, appliance, government procurement rails, MAS/IMDA mapping), and fourteen years of regional security heritage.

Where we win — steer here

  • · Data residency / sovereignty is a stated requirement (government, FSI, critical infrastructure)
  • · Buyer must evidence agent governance to a regulator — MAS SAFR, IMDA framework, internal audit
  • · On-prem, customer-cloud or air-gapped deployment is mandatory or preferred
  • · Buying committee includes public-sector procurement (framework rails, local entity, local support)
  • · The CISO has been burned by "risk scores" before and wants proof of exploitability

Where we lose — qualify hard, or walk

  • · US-headquartered buyer running a global consolidation play on an existing CNAPP/cloud-security platform
  • · Pure developer-tool evaluation (an eval/testing library shortlist, no governance requirement)
  • · Buyer wants a global brand logo for board optics more than fit — let the incumbent win the logo contest
  • · No agent deployments in production and none planned within 12 months (park in nurture, revisit)

Losing fast in the wrong deals funds winning slow in the right ones.

Landmines to plant early (fair, factual, fatal)

  • "Where — physically — will your agent telemetry and incident evidence be stored, and under whose legal jurisdiction?"
  • "When their platform scores a risk 'critical', ask what evidence proves it's actually exploitable in your environment."
  • "Ask for their MAS SAFR and IMDA agentic-framework mapping. Ask how many regulated APAC deployments they'll reference."
  • "Ask whether the air-gapped option is on the price list or on the roadmap."

Objection handling

  • "We've never heard of CloudsineAI." — "Fourteen years securing this region's web layer; 41 customers across 5 countries; 100 GenAI applications secured in one public-sector deployment. The name is new to the category. The security DNA isn't."
  • "The big platform says they'll cover agents soon." — "Roadmaps don't produce audit evidence. MAS SAFR is in force now; we map to it today, in-country."
  • "Rivals have an attack graph too." — "They do — we never claimed otherwise. Ask which of them attaches closed-loop red-team evidence to each path, and stores it inside your jurisdiction."
  • "You're small — what if you get acquired?" — "Our platform runs in your tenancy or on your appliance; your evidence and your controls stay yours under any scenario. Ask the SaaS-only vendors the same question."

Discovery questions that open the gap

  • "How many AI agents are running in your environment right now? How confident are you in that number?"
  • "If your regulator asked tomorrow what a specific agent did last Tuesday — tools invoked, data touched — how long would the answer take?"
  • "Who signed off the blast radius of your most privileged agent?"
  • "What happens to your AI programme the day after an agent incident? Who gets to switch it back on, and on what evidence?"
